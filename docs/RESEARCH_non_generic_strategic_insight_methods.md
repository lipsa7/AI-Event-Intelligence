# Forcing choices turns news into strategy

Analysts and strategists avoid generic output by building their process around constraints, not by being cleverer. Before they write a recommendation, they name the crux, force a choice between real alternatives, write down what would have to be true, model other actors from those actors' own incentives, and commit to a falsifiable call. That call carries a calibrated likelihood, the alternatives that were rejected, and indicators that would prove it wrong. An LLM pipeline needs the same scaffolding, because the main failure is structural, not a lack of knowledge. A 2026 HBR study found that **7 leading LLMs almost uniformly chose the fashionable option (differentiation, augmentation, long-term) on classic strategic tensions regardless of company context**, and **adding detailed context reduced the bias only modestly** ([UDE summary](https://www.uni-due.de/biwi/vet/ki-labor/moreaboutclusterb.php); [NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/research-highlights/researchers-asked-llms-strategic-advice-they-got-trendslop-return.md)). Commercial intelligence products mostly don't solve this at the reasoning layer. Their "Recommendations" sections are template slots, made specific by the customer's own data and prompts. The interventions with the strongest evidence for an LLM pipeline are explicit K-level opponent modelling, overgenerate-dedupe-tournament ranking, and checks against external criteria in place of self-critique. Forced binary choices, unfashionable-side steelmanning, swap tests and ICD 203-style output schemas are well-grounded designs, but their effect on strategy output has not been measured. A prioritized build list closes the report.

## "Meh" output is a structural bias that better context and better models do not fix

The trendslop study is the most direct evidence on why LLM strategy advice reads as generic. Romasanta, Thomas and Levina tested 7 leading models on 7 classic tensions: exploration/exploitation, centralisation/decentralisation, short/long term, competition/collaboration, radical/incremental, differentiation/commodification and automation/augmentation. They varied company context and prompt wording. Across "thousands of simulations" the models picked the same trendy side. **Cost leadership was systematically overlooked, augmentation beat automation, and long-term beat short-term even when short-term action was critical for survival.** Only exploration/exploitation varied by model ([UDE summary](https://www.uni-due.de/biwi/vet/ki-labor/moreaboutclusterb.php)). When the models were not forced to choose, they defaulted to **hybrid answers such as "differentiation plus cost leadership"**, which the authors flag as the classic "stuck in the middle" error. The authors' untested explanation is that training data attaches positive sentiment to the fashionable terms. Specifics come second-hand: HBR could not be fetched, and no working paper with per-tension numbers was found.

The pattern matches what strategy theory calls bad strategy. Rumelt's hallmarks are fluff, failure to face the challenge (leaving "a stretch goal, a budget, or a list of things you wish would happen"), mistaking goals for strategy, and impracticable objectives ([Brikman](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2023/01/18/good-strategy-bad-strategy/)). Roger Martin names the opposite of good strategy as **indifference to choice**, with the word "and" as its marker ([Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-roger-l-martin/)). An LLM recommending "differentiate and stay cost-competitive while investing in partnerships" fails all of these tests at once. Upgrading the model will not fix it. Trendslop held across all seven frontier models. Explicit opponent modelling added no significant gain for GPT-4o on SOTOPIA-hard (3.59 vs 3.54) but a large one for Llama-3.1-70B (3.52 vs 2.90), so scaffolding matters most where the model is weakest ([arXiv 2402.01521](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01521v2)). No head-to-head study of reasoning versus non-reasoning models on strategy quality was found. The defensible conclusion is that pipeline structure, not model choice, is the lever.

Intelligence tradecraft diagnoses the same problem in human analysts. Heuer writes that "mind-sets tend to be quick to form but resistant to change," and that disproving a hypothesis takes far more information than forming one ([Heuer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The CIA Tradecraft Primer names anchoring, overconfidence and overweighting easily recalled events as the drivers, and treats structured techniques as the remedy because they make mental models explicit and expose alternatives ([Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)). An LLM's prior toward fashionable answers works like a mindset at scale, and the same procedural countermeasures apply.

## Professionals name the crux and the conditions before they recommend anything

Every strong tradition in the notes turns a recommendation into a chain of explicit, testable parts. Rumelt's kernel is **diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent actions**. The guiding policy must rule actions out, and the actions must reinforce one another ([Brikman](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2023/01/18/good-strategy-bad-strategy/)). His crux is "the hardest problem you face that you can actually do something about." His Strategy Foundry process generates 8–10 challenges and winnows them to the one that is addressable. It starts from challenges rather than goals, because goals "short circuit the logic" ([BCG Henderson Institute](https://bcghendersoninstitute.com/the-crux-with-richard-rumelt/)). His grocery example shows what specificity looks like. Facing a 24/7 discount rival, the diagnosis is that nearby customers split into price-sensitive students and time-poor professionals. The guiding policy is to serve the professionals, and the actions are a second checkout, more parking and prepared healthy meals. Each element is particular to that store.

Martin's "What Would Have to Be True" is the most portable anti-generic device across the traditions. Frame a choice, generate possibilities, specify the conditions under which each would be right, identify barriers, then test. The person most skeptical of a condition designs its test ([Inside Higher Ed on Lafley & Martin](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/stratedgy/possibilities)). The IC's **Key Assumptions Check** has the same structure. State the analytic line, list every stated and unstated premise, challenge each, and keep the load-bearing ones together with what would invalidate them ([Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)). Helmer adds a test for any claimed advantage: real power needs both a **benefit** and a **barrier** that stops rivals arbitraging it away, such as counter-positioning, where the incumbent cannot copy without damaging its own business ([Samek summary of 7 Powers](https://evansamek.substack.com/p/summary-7-powers)). Wack's scenario work separates **predetermined elements** ("events that have already occurred but whose consequences have not yet unfolded") from true uncertainties ([Strathclyde](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/4284/)). That separation fits news-driven analysis especially well, because most events already in the news are predetermined elements whose consequences are still unfolding.

ICD 203 sets the output standard. Analysts must express uncertainty and *explain its basis*, keep intelligence apart from assumptions and judgments, analyze alternatives, address implications for the customer, flag changes from prior analysis, and, in effect an anti-hedging rule, "not avoid difficult judgments in order to minimize the risk of being wrong." It fixes a probability lexicon (likely = 55–80%, very likely = 80–95%, and so on) and **forbids putting a likelihood term and a confidence level in the same sentence** ([ICD 203](https://irp.fas.org/dni/icd/icd-203.pdf)). Taken together, these define a good key judgment: a specific, falsifiable claim about an actor, a lexicon likelihood, a separate confidence statement with its basis, 1–3 load-bearing assumptions each paired with what would overturn it, the strongest rejected alternative, indicators, and the customer "so what." A weak judgment is hedged ("could," "may"), unfalsifiable and tied to no decision.

The notes contain no efficacy data showing that structured analytic techniques improve forecast accuracy. That literature is contested and was not retrieved. The evidence for pre-mortems is the most concrete. Prospective hindsight produced about 30% more reasons for failure, though their quality was not measured. Pre-mortems cut overconfidence about twice as much as pros/cons lists, and pre-mortem teams surfaced more risks than brainstorming teams ([Jason Collins](https://corporate.jcx.au/premortem)).

## Good calls read as forecasts, name rejected scenarios, and get graded

Human intelligence firms make their judgments checkable. An Oxford Analytica brief uses a forecast as its headline ("Russia's influence in the Middle East will decline"), gives a "What next" bottom line, and adds "Subsidiary Impacts" bullets that each name an actor and a direction. Examples: "Iran is unlikely to receive significant volumes of advanced weaponry from Russia," and "Moscow will prioritise ties with Saudi Arabia and the Gulf states" ([Oxford Analytica sample](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/meetings/external/annualmeeting-1/annualmeeting-2/Oxford-Analytica-WB-IMF-25%20v3%20copy.pdf)). Eurasia Group's Top Risks pairs each coined risk with a one-sentence thesis and publishes a separate **"Red Herrings"** section of plausible scenarios it judged unlikely. In 2025 these included "the energy transition stalls" ([Eurasia Group](https://www.eurasiagroup.net/issues/top-risks-2025)). The Stratfor/GPF lineage reasons from **imperatives** (what an actor must do to survive and prosper) and **constraints** (what it cannot do), on the premise that "individual leaders rarely change the outcome" ([GPF](https://geopoliticalfutures.com/understanding-model)). RANE/Stratfor grades its own forecasts publicly and calls grading "perhaps the most necessary thing we do" ([RANE](https://worldview.ranenetwork.com/content/analysis/2020-12-08/how-grading-our-work-makes-us-better-forecasters)). One outside test of risk lists adds a sharper bar: a valuable call is one judged more likely than not that consensus rates as much less probable ([The Gray Rhino](https://www.thegrayrhino.com/what-to-look-for-in-a-top-risks-list-part-ii/)).

Consulting reaches the same shape through Minto's pyramid. The governing thought comes first. It is framed by Situation–Complication–Question, where the Complication is "the 'so what' of the problem," and each level summarizes the MECE group below it ([Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/pyramid-principle)). The imperatives-and-constraints framing maps directly onto the pipeline's "incentives/constraints" field. Red Herrings map onto the rejected-alternative requirement in ICD 203. None of the AI products reviewed showed calibrated probabilities, rejected scenarios or forecast grading in their documentation. That gap separates the best human products from current AI tooling.

## Other players must be simulated from their incentives, not from personas

Opponent modelling is the best-evidenced single technique for the "other players' moves → best move" part of the chain. K-Level Reasoning works in two steps: predict each rival's move at level k−1, then best-respond. In the 0.8-of-average guessing game, GPT-4 with K-R reached a **0.65 win rate, against 0.32 for CoT, 0.41 for persona prompting, 0.37 for Self-Refine and 0.16 for direct answers**. One table inside the paper reports CoT at 0.42 instead. K-R also won in survival auctions and negotiation ([arXiv 2402.01521](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01521v2)). The gain comes from modelling, not extra compute: Self-Refine used more tokens (152k vs 125k) and did worse. Depth should match the opponent. K=3 beat K=2 against sophisticated opponents (0.60 vs 0.52) but did slightly worse against naive ones (0.77 vs 0.82).

The wargaming evidence sets the limits. Lamparth et al. compared 214 national-security experts with GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and GPT-4o. **The LLMs matched human action frequencies on only about half of 21 actions** (GPT-4o on 9). They were more path-locked: after a de-escalatory first move, they chose an aggressive second move with probability 0.73–0.86, against 0.65 for humans. **Labelling players "strict pacifists" or "aggressive sociopaths" produced no significant change** ([arXiv 2403.03407](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03407v4)). Personas also failed elsewhere: across 162 roles and 2,410 questions they gave no improvement over no persona ([arXiv 2311.10054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054v2)). The design implication is precise. Run each rival as a separate call fed with that rival's own retrieved incentives, constraints and recent moves, not a character label. Ask for 2–3 alternative moves with probabilities rather than one prediction, because LLM rivals are less varied than real ones. Then generate the focal actor's move as a best response. This fits the IC Red Team's first-person method against mirror-imaging ([Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)). It also fits Brandenburger and Nalebuff's PARTS, which adds complementors and "tactics" to the player map. In their Netscape example, a price war against a deeper-pocketed rival lost where signalling a niche focus might have allowed coexistence ([Think Insights, PARTS](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/parts)).

## Commercial tools win on inputs and denoising, not on reasoning

Established products mostly don't reason their way to recommendations. Feedly's AI Actions fill templates such as "Report: ... Executive Summary, Key Takeaways, Recommendations, and Conclusion" over 10–25 user-selected articles, with inline citations on every part of the response. Company context is whatever the user writes into the prompt: role, audience, goal, competitor restrictions and table schemas ([Feedly docs](https://docs.feedly.com/article/741-guide-to-ai-actions-for-feedly-market-intelligence); [Feedly prompts](https://docs.feedly.com/article/740-writing-effective-prompts-for-ai-actions-market-intelligence)). AlphaSense Deep Research builds a reviewable research plan and coordinates sub-agents for retrieval, quantitative data, analysis and synthesis. Each insight links to its source with the passage highlighted, and when nothing relevant is found the system says so rather than generating ([AlphaSense](https://help.alpha-sense.com/hc/en-us/articles/41666587181203-Interacting-with-Generative-Search)). Even Dataminr, with real-time event detection over 1M+ sources, listed "customer-tailored impact assessments, and recommended customer actions" as roadmap items ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/04/18/dataminr-launches-regenai-enhance-real-time-event-analysis-generative-ai/); [Dataminr](https://www.dataminr.com/press/dataminr-unveils-agentic-ai-roadmap)). Org-specific "what you should do next" is therefore not a solved product capability. It depends on an explicit profile of each actor.

Klue gives the clearest account of why naive RAG output is generic. Its failure modes are equal weighting of vendor blogs, verified buyer quotes and stale articles; broken or outdated citations shown with full confidence; and "retrieval variance," where rephrasing a question changes the talking points. The root cause it names is raw data going straight to the LLM. Its fix is an intelligence layer: classify signals, score them by **source credibility, specificity and cross-deal frequency**, route them, discard noise, synthesize recurring patterns, and only then generate ([Klue](https://klue.com/blog/ai-battlecard)). This is vendor content, but it is concrete, and it matches the IC's Quality of Information Check: grade sources, check corroboration and re-test judgments built on retracted reporting ([Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)). For a news pipeline, retrieval variance argues for persisting a canonical cited fact base per actor and reasoning over that, rather than re-retrieving on every query.

## External checks and tournaments beat asking the model to critique itself

Two tempting quality fixes have negative evidence. LLMs "struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback," and sometimes get worse after trying ([arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798v2)). Self-Refine and Reflect underperformed plain CoT in the K-R games ([arXiv 2402.01521](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01521v2)). Critique therefore has to run against external, checkable criteria. Does every claim cite a fact ID? Is every condition falsifiable? Does every action have an owner and a timeframe? Does the recommendation survive an **actor-swap test**, or would it read the same for any firm in the sector? The swap test and the "opposite is not absurd" test come from Rumelt and Martin, but they are design inferences that have not been validated as metrics.

For generating the best move, overgenerating and then ranking has good evidence for novelty. Si, Yang and Hashimoto generated 4,000 seed ideas per topic, deduplicated at cosine 0.8 with MiniLM embeddings, and ranked them with a Swiss-system pairwise tournament. **The AI ideas were rated more novel than expert ideas (5.64 vs 4.84, p<0.05) with no significant feasibility loss.** But only about 5% (~200) were non-duplicates, and the unique count plateaued ([arXiv 2409.04109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.04109)). That plateau argues for 10–30 candidates generated under deliberately different lenses (each tension side, each power type, each rival response), not thousands of samples. On evaluation, expectations should be modest. Human reviewers agreed on only 56% of pairwise idea judgments, and the best LLM judge reached 53.3% agreement with humans ([arXiv 2409.04109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.04109)). A rubric-scoring LLM averaged over 3 runs correlated **0.52 with VC judges on business plans, higher than VCs agreed with each other (ICC 0.51 vs 0.25)**, but only **0.21 on innovation and 0.24 on product**, which are exactly the non-obvious-move dimensions ([arXiv 2408.08811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08811)). Judges are reliable on structured, checkable sub-criteria and unreliable on "is this insightful." Scoring should be decomposed accordingly.

## Mapping the methods onto each pipeline stage

| Pipeline stage | Professional method to encode | What blocks generic output |
|---|---|---|
| News → cited facts | Quality of Information Check; Klue scoring layer | Credibility, recency and corroboration score per fact; weak sources dropped before generation; persisted fact IDs |
| Crux | Rumelt crux and Strategy Foundry; SCQ complication | Generate 8–10 challenges, winnow to one *addressable* one; reject a crux that is a goal |
| Leverage | Helmer benefit + barrier; PARTS added value | Leverage must name both a benefit and why rivals can't copy it |
| Incentives / constraints | Stratfor imperatives and constraints; Outside-In STEEP | Separate "must do" from "cannot do"; separate predetermined elements from uncertainties |
| Other players' moves | K-level reasoning; Red Team; wargaming | One call per rival using its own facts; 2–3 moves with probabilities; no persona labels |
| Best move + trade-offs | Martin choice framing; forced binary per tension; overgenerate-and-rank | Pick one side per tension, steelman the unfashionable side, reject unqualified "both" |
| Conditions | WWHTBT; Key Assumptions Check; indicators | 1–3 load-bearing conditions, each falsifiable, with signposts and a time window |
| Actions | Rumelt coherent actions; guiding policy | State what will *not* be done; actions reinforce each other; owner and timeframe |
| Output | ICD 203; Oxford Analytica; Eurasia Red Herrings | Forecast headline, lexicon likelihood, separate confidence, rejected alternative, change since last run |

## Prioritized implementation list

Evidence strength uses four levels. **Strong** means controlled experiments directly on the mechanism. **Moderate** means empirical evidence from an adjacent task. **Doctrine** means established professional practice with no efficacy data in the notes. **Design** means a reasoned inference, not yet tested.

| # | Build | Why first / what it fixes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Per-actor cited fact base with source scoring.** Extract atomic facts with IDs, source, date, credibility tier and corroboration count; drop low-score items; persist them per actor and reuse across runs. | Every later stage depends on it. Fixes equal source weighting, stale citations and retrieval variance. | Moderate (Klue failure modes, IC QoI Check, AlphaSense/Feedly citation standard) |
| 2 | **Structured output schema modelled on ICD 203 / Oxford Analytica.** Forecast-style headline; per claim `fact_ids[]`; lexicon likelihood; a separate confidence field with its basis; `rejected_alternative`; `changes_vs_prior`. Validate the schema in code. | Turns the "so what" from prose into checkable fields. Bans un-cited claims and bare "could/may." | Doctrine (ICD 203, Oxford Analytica, Eurasia Red Herrings) |
| 3 | **Crux step as challenge generation plus winnowing.** Generate 8–10 challenges for the actor from its facts, score each for addressability and stakes, pick one, and reject goal-shaped cruxes. | Stops "grow / innovate / differentiate" from standing in for a diagnosis. | Doctrine (Rumelt) |
| 4 | **Forced choice per strategic tension plus steelman of the unfashionable side.** Pick 2–3 relevant tensions; require a choice with a dominance condition; generate the strongest case for cost leadership, automation or the short term before deciding. | The direct countermeasure to trendslop, which context alone barely fixes. | Design, recommended by the trendslop authors; effect size unmeasured |
| 5 | **K=2 rival simulation.** A separate call per key rival, fed with that rival's facts, imperatives and constraints, returning 2–3 moves with probabilities; the focal best move is generated as a best response. Use K=3 only for sophisticated actors such as states and large incumbents. | Fills "other players' moves" with modelled responses instead of mirror-imaging. | Strong for games (K-R); Moderate caveat (LLM rivals diverge from experts, personas ignored) |
| 6 | **External-check critic, replacing self-critique.** Code-level checks: every claim has a fact ID, every condition is falsifiable and has a signpost and window, every action has an owner and timeframe, a "will not do" line exists, and there is no unqualified "and." Add an **actor-swap test**: rerun on a swapped actor and flag recommendations that barely change. | Self-critique without external signal is unreliable; these checks are cheap and deterministic. | Moderate for "external over intrinsic" (Huang et al.); Design for swap test |
| 7 | **WWHTBT / Key Assumptions block with signposts.** 1–3 load-bearing conditions, each with what would overturn it and an observable indicator; separate predetermined elements from uncertainties. | Makes the call falsifiable and gives the next run something to monitor. | Doctrine (Martin, KAC, Wack, IC indicators) |
| 8 | **Overgenerate, dedupe, tournament for the best move.** 10–30 candidates across varied lenses (each tension side, each power type, each rival response); embedding dedupe at about 0.8 cosine; Swiss pairwise ranking on specificity, grounding, trade-off clarity and feasibility. | Gives real alternatives to choose between and feeds the rejected-alternative field. | Strong for novelty (Si et al.); diversity plateau caps N |
| 9 | **Pre-mortem and devil's-advocate pass on the chosen move,** run against the fact base, with outcomes confirm, revise or caveat. | Cuts overconfidence and surfaces risks the forward pass missed. | Moderate (pre-mortem studies); Doctrine (IC Devil's Advocacy) |
| 10 | **Eval harness.** Pairwise judging, order-swapped, 3+ runs averaged, scored on decomposed sub-criteria; validated against a small human-rated set targeting about 55–65% agreement. | Measures whether changes 3–9 actually reduce genericness. | Moderate (Si et al., Csaszar et al.) |
| 11 | **Forecast ledger and grading.** Store each judgment with likelihood and signposts; on later runs, mark it hit, miss or open; show "change vs prior." | The RANE/Stratfor practice that turns analysis into a track record; also ICD 203 standard 7. | Doctrine (RANE, ICD 203) |
| 12 | **Model ensembling across families** for the rival-move and condition-probability steps, ahead of switching models wholesale. | Bias is shared across frontier models; ensembles plus retrieval lead ForecastBench. | Moderate (FRI ForecastBench; trendslop cross-model result) |

Items 1–2 are infrastructure, so they come first even though their evidence is not the strongest. Items 4–6 are the core anti-generic interventions and should be built together. Persona prompts ("you are an aggressive CEO") and free-form "critique and improve your answer" loops should be dropped explicitly, because both have negative evidence.

## Conclusion

The notes support a shift in where the pipeline invests its effort. Quality comes from what the model is *not allowed* to do: recommend both sides of a tension, cite nothing, hedge without a probability, ignore how rivals respond, or produce advice that would read the same for a different company. Those prohibitions can mostly be checked by code rather than by a model's taste. That matters because LLM judges are weakest exactly where "insight" lives, with innovation scoring at r ≈ 0.21. The professional traditions agree with each other more than their vocabularies suggest. Rumelt's crux, Martin's conditions, the IC's assumptions check and Stratfor's constraints are the same move: make the load-bearing premises explicit and falsifiable.

The open frontier is also a differentiation opportunity. No documented AI intelligence product ships calibrated likelihoods, named red herrings or graded forecast history, and even Dataminr treats client-tailored actions as roadmap. A pipeline that persists its judgments and grades them against later news would have something none of the reviewed tools document: a measured record of whether its "what to do next" calls were right. The main uncertainty is that, apart from K-level reasoning and overgenerate-and-rank, the interventions here are well-founded designs whose effect on strategy output has not been measured. The eval harness in item 10 is how to find out which ones earn their cost.
