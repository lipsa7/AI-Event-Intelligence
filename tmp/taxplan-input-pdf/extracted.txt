Summary
Only 82 of 5,701 leads (1.4%) have paid, while 817 abandoned carts worth ₹47.3 lakh sit
unworked. The page shows who the leads are, but not how fast they were contacted, by
whom (AI or human), or what happened next. Data covers 17 May to 27 Oct 2026 (export
of 27 Oct).
Top findings:
The revenue card under-reports. "Paid revenue ₹48,494" counts only the 21 payments
made through WhatsApp checkout or an agent-sent link. 61 more leads in this same
WhatsApp admin paid on the website (about ₹81,400), so revenue from these leads is
about ₹1.30 lakh. If the card is meant to be WhatsApp-only, it should say so and show
website payments beside it.
The bot loses most leads in its first two questions. 35% of leads stop at language
selection and 32% at service selection.
Meta ads bring 78% of leads but convert at 0.4%. Direct traffic converts at 2.4% and
the website widget at 5.5%.
Rider ads route riders to the wrong product. The system files 77% of leads from the
Swiggy/Zomato/Zepto TDS-refund ads under the business "TDS" category, and more of
them then picked "TDS Monthly Payment" (126) than the ₹599 rider ITR filing (99).
Follow-up is close to absent. 57% of leads were never touched after entry, only 1 lead
has a next follow-up date, and 206 leads have waited in human handoff for a median of
91 days.
AI calling has just started and is not visible. Only 5 leads carry a voice_agent source,
and the export has no call log, so the assignment questions (AI vs human call, time to
first AI call) cannot yet be answered.

Funnel: where the bot loses leads
The bot's own questions cause the two biggest losses: 40% of leads quit at the language
question, and 73% of those who pick a service never reach a price.
Language step. 2,013 leads stop here. Most Meta ads are in English and the headline
already names the service, so asking language first adds a step before any value is
shown. Auto-detect language from the first message, or ask it later.
Service step. 1,804 leads stop at the service list (27 services in 6 categories). Ads
should deep-link straight to their service, so a rider from a TDS ad lands on rider ITR,
not a menu.
Checkout. Of 299 leads sent a checkout link, 211 have payment status "cancelled".
These are the proven-intent audience the assignment's cart-recovery stats point to.

Sources, ads and services
Meta ads fill the pipeline but rarely pay: 4,426 leads gave 18 payments, against 15
payments from just 274 widget leads.
Ads. Of 34 ad headlines, only 5 produced any payment. "Your Swiggy TDS Can Come
Back" brought 1,147 leads and 326 carts but 4 payments. "ITR Filing for Freelancers" (355
leads) and "50% Off Business ITR Filing" (338) produced none. Without ad spend in the
data, cost per paid lead cannot be computed yet; that is the first thing GA4 and Meta
conversion sync should fix.
The rider mismatch. Of 1,187 leads from the three rider TDS-refund ads, 912 (77%) were
put in the "TDS" category. The system sets this from the ad, not the lead: 335 rider-ad
leads already carry "TDS" while still at the language question. 126 then picked "TDS
Monthly Payment", a business compliance product with a median cart of ₹2,355. Only 99
found "ITR Filing for Delivery & Ride Partners" (₹599), the product the ad actually sells. Of
the 336 rider-ad leads who picked a service, more chose the business product than the
rider product, and the three ads produced 4 payments from 1,187 leads. One thing to
confirm with the team: if "TDS Monthly Payment" is meant for riders, the problem is its
name, not the routing. Korea's Samjjeomsam, a tax-refund app for gig workers, solves the
same problem by showing the refund amount first and the fee second.
Services. ITR Salary Filing is the workhorse: 321 leads, 306 carts, 28 payments. TDS (1,167
leads, 188 carts, median cart ₹2,355 to ₹14,155) has 0 payments, and GSTR has 2 payments
from 372 leads. The higher-value services need a human or a consultation path, not self-

Season. 79% of leads and 92% of payments came in July and August (ITR deadlines).
October has 246 leads and 0 payments, so the off-season is the time to fix the funnel
before the next peak.
Speed to lead, AI calls, follow-ups and carts
The assignment asks which calls are AI and which are human, and how soon leads get an
AI call; the current page and export cannot answer either.
What can be measured today.
Calls. The export has no call log, no call type (AI or human), no first-contact time and
no outcome. Only 5 leads (all since 15 Oct) carry source "voice_agent", and all are still
at new_contact. The "My Calls" tab exists, but nothing about calls reaches the lead row
or the export.
Touch. For 57% of leads (3,227), "last contacted" is within 5 minutes of creation: the
bot's opening message was the only contact. For 27%, the last contact came more than
a day later.
Follow-ups. Only 1 of 5,701 leads has a next follow-up date (set for 27 Oct, 5:38 pm). In
the raw file the column headed "Next follow-up" looks full, but every value is in the past
and equals the lead's creation time; because of the shift, the real follow-up field is the
one headed "Last contacted", and it holds that single date.
Handoffs. 206 leads sit in human_handoff (111) or handoff_pending (95). The median
wait since last contact is 91 days and none were closed. The assignment's warm-
handoff benchmark is a transfer that completes with context; here most handoffs
never complete.
Why it matters. Per the assignment's sources, a lead is 21x more likely to qualify when
contacted at 5 minutes than at 30 (MIT/InsideSales), and AI call plus WhatsApp converts
2.5x better than one channel (GoKwik). With 36% of leads arriving at night and 57% never
touched again, an instant AI call is the cheapest lift available.

Benchmark: how it was built
300 distinct firms were benchmarked, 15 in each of 20 countries, against 15 capabilities a
leads page can offer. The full firm-by-feature matrix is in the companion spreadsheet
(Lead_Page_Benchmark_300_firms.xlsx).
Countries (20): India, USA, Canada, UK, Germany, France, Spain, Brazil, Mexico,
Indonesia, Vietnam, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Australia, Japan, South Korea, China,
Turkey, South Africa. They cover the biggest CRM markets, the WhatsApp-first
markets (India, Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia), and the markets where chat runs on LINE,
Kakao, Zalo or WeChat.
Firms (15 per country): local leaders first, then firms that are major in that market. Five
kinds of firm are mixed on purpose, because the page does five jobs: CRM and sales
tools (72), WhatsApp and chat platforms (63), voice AI and contact centres (55), tax,
accounting and compliance apps that sell services like this business (52), and
payments, marketing-data and vertical lead platforms (58).
Scoring: each firm is marked 1 or 0 per capability, based on what its public product
material advertises as of late 2026. Each firm starts from its segment's typical feature
set and is then adjusted for firm-specific features known from its product pages.
Confidence: 59 firms are rated High (widely documented products); 241 are Medium.
Treat the matrix as a desk-research map of the market, and spot-check any single cell
before quoting it externally.
Benchmark: what the market offers
Across 300 firms, the leads page lacks or only partly has 14 of the 15 capabilities (consent
is not visible either way), including the four that 95–100% of lead-management tools treat
as standard.

=== PAGE 9 ===
Table stakes (fix first). Routing rules, automated follow-up cadences and a single timeline
of WhatsApp, call and email activity are near-universal in CRMs and WhatsApp tools,
from LeadSquared and Kylas in India to Kommo in Mexico and Qontak in Indonesia. Funnel
analytics follows at 71%.
Differentiators (where this page can lead). Instant AI calling, AI-vs-human call logs,
speed-to-lead timers and best-time-to-call analytics are rare in lead tools (8–27%). They
are common only in voice-AI and contact-centre products, so few CRMs combine them.
That gap is the opening: a leads page that shows, per lead, "AI called in 2 min, no answer;
human called at 11:10, cart sent".

India and the USA lead on AI calling (40% and 33% of firms benchmarked offer instant
AI calls), driven by SquadStack, Exotel, Ozonetel, 11x, Bland AI and Salesforce
Agentforce.
Mexico and Indonesia lead on cart recovery and handoff (73% offer cart recovery,
67–80% bot-to-human handoff). In these WhatsApp-first markets, the chat thread is
the lead record, as in Kommo, Leadsales and Qontak.
Korea shows the closest analog to the rider funnel. Samjjeomsam and Toss lead with
the refund amount a gig worker can claim, then charge a fee.
Japan, Korea, Vietnam and China run the same pattern on LINE, Kakao, Zalo and
WeCom instead of WhatsApp, which confirms the chat-first design but shows no
market has solved AI-call visibility on the lead row.
Germany, the UK and Turkey stand out on consent: GDPR or IYS consent is recorded
on the lead itself. India's DPDP Act and TRAI DND rules make the same thing relevant
here.
Checked on vendor pages (research pass, 27 Oct): LeadSquared offers a built-in voice
bot (Invorto); Respond.io offers AI voice agents and records the Meta ad click ID
(ctwa_clid) automatically; Qontak shows SLA countdowns on chats; Interakt, Gallabox
and Kommo send conversions back to Meta. No platform found combines a speed-to-lead
timer, an AI-vs-human call log and a value-tiered cart queue on one leads page, so building
those together would be a genuine differentiator. Cells in the spreadsheet not checked on
vendor pages remain desk-research estimates.

WhatsApp message rules to plan around. A lead arriving from a click-to-WhatsApp ad
opens a free messaging window (72 hours under Meta's long-standing rule; some 2026
BSP guides say longer). Since 1 October 2026, service messages (free-form replies inside
the 24-hour window) are no longer all free: the first 1,000 per number per month are free
and every reply after that costs the utility rate (₹0.115 plus 18% GST, before BSP markup).
Marketing templates rose from ₹0.7846 to ₹0.8631 on 1 January 2026. Confirm both on
Meta's rate card before building cadences around them.
Compliance before scaling AI calls
AI calls to people who never asked for a call are the riskiest part of the new voice
programme.
TRAI (Feb 2025 TCCCPR amendment): ordinary 10-digit numbers can no longer be
used for telemarketing. Promotional calls go on the 140 series and transactional calls on
1600; first violations carry a ₹2 lakh penalty. A July 2026 clarification reportedly limits
1600 to regulated financial entities and government, which would put this business's
sales calls on DLT-registered 140 numbers. Confirm with the telecom provider.
DPDP Rules 2025: notified 13 Nov 2025; full notice and consent duties apply from 13
May 2027. Consent notices must be standalone and name the specific purpose.
