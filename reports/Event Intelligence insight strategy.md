# Label judgments instead of deleting them

Event Intelligence is not short of models, sources or prompts. Its main problem is how it checks claims. Every precedent, leverage claim and hidden connection the product exists to produce is graded by asking **"does a source state this?"** Sources report what happened, not what an actor should do next. So **all 20 reports since 9 Oct ended "abstained" or near-empty, with zero hidden insights after 5 Oct**, and expert commentary and analogies were almost always blank ([data/reports.sqlite3](data/reports.sqlite3)). Professional analysis solves this differently. ICD 203 *requires* analysts to make difficult judgments that go beyond their sources. It controls the risk with labels: source fact, assumption, judgment, separate likelihood and confidence, and indicators that would change the call ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). Commercial tools (AlphaSense, Klue, Dataminr, Recorded Future) stop at cited summaries. None publicly offers labelled original strategic judgment, so this is real white space, but only if inference is checked as inference. Four things keep the app from its goal:
- a verification design that contradicts the objective;
- two weeks of tuning on one replayed 63-event checkpoint;
- research that loses material (41% fetch failures, off-target precedent queries, focus research that arrives too late);
- no human ground truth at all.

The fix is to change the claim-checking design, not to add sources: source the premises, check the inferential step for validity, and give it a visible label. Next comes fresh data and a small human-rated set. One stop matters as much: stop adding gates in a review-and-bump loop.

## The objective needs three typed outputs, each with a traceable skeleton

The product spec asks for three forms of forward reasoning: precedent ("because this happened last time, this can happen to this company"), leverage plus incentives ("current rules give actor X leverage, so move Y is right"), and hidden cross-actor links ([docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md:4-10](docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md)). Professional practice offers a concrete template for each. All three share one move: **source the endpoints, and make the link itself an explicitly labelled judgment.**

**Precedent should be built in two tiers.** Forecasting research says to start from the outside view. Find a reference class "broad enough to be statistically meaningful but narrow enough to be truly comparable," then place the case in that class's distribution of outcomes ([Flyvbjerg 2006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.3642)). In the Good Judgment Project, forecasts tagged as using comparison classes averaged a **Brier score of 0.17 against 0.26** for the next-best approach ([AI Impacts GJP synthesis](https://aiimpacts.org/?p=1283)). When only one analog exists, Heuer's warning applies: single-analogy inference is "more prone to error than most other forms of inference," and is best used "to suggest hypotheses and to highlight differences" ([Heuer 1999](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The 2026 *Studies in Intelligence* method fits that case. It sorts the facts into Known, Unclear and Presumed, then fills in a similarities–differences table that marks each critical element as analogous, weak or not analogous ([Handy 2026](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/6-Analogical_Reasoning_in_Intelligence_Analysis.pdf)). For the app, this means a sourced precedent renders as an "analog-suggested hypothesis" with a stated key difference and confidence capped at Moderate. Where N comparable cases exist, it upgrades to "in X of N cases, outcome O followed." Retrieval matters too: LLM agents tend to match analogies on surface features rather than mechanism ([ADR benchmark, 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.13602.md)). Precedents should therefore be pulled by actor and mechanism, not by embedding similarity alone. That is what the existing proposal in [docs/DESIGN_memory_pass.md](docs/DESIGN_memory_pass.md) does: it feeds dated earlier events from `event_intelligence.db` and `event_memory` into the lab, with leakage rules.

**Leverage reasoning has a standard skeleton.** Fisher and Ury identify the best alternative to a negotiated agreement (BATNA), not money or influence, as the "principal source of power" ([BATNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_alternative_to_a_negotiated_agreement)). Brandenburger and Nalebuff's PARTS framework (players, added value, rules, tactics, scope) treats the game as something a player can reshape ([PARTS summary](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/parts)). A traceable leverage claim therefore has five parts:
1. Each party's alternatives, which are sourced.
2. What X controls that others cannot replace, which is sourced.
3. The specific rule or deadline that shifts those alternatives, quoted from the rule itself.
4. Move Y, which is the analytic judgment.
5. A red-team check of how the counterparty answers, written from the counterparty's point of view to avoid mirror-imaging ([CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)).

Move Y is validated by robustness and falsifiability, not by a source saying so. The analyst tests it across 2–4 scenarios, runs a pre-mortem, and lists indicators. Pierre Wack's "predetermined elements" are the richest source of non-obvious claims that still carry support. These are facts that are already reported, such as a rule taking effect on a known date, whose consequences have not yet unfolded ([Burt on Wack](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/id/eprint/4284)). The pending **lab_dependency_patch** in the repo root (`event_intelligence_lab_dependency_patch.zip`) matches this template. It splits leverage into a *sourced control claim* and an *inferred dependency claim*, which is the separation the frameworks call for. The audit did not inspect the patch contents. They should be checked against the template before installing.

**Hidden connections fit Klein's "connection path".** Klein's 120-case model finds that insight arises from connections, contradictions and creative desperation ([Gary Klein](https://gary-klein.com/insight)). For the app, both endpoints of a link must be sourced. The link is the judgment, and it must name its mechanism: a shared regulator, supplier, rule, counterparty or correlated exposure. STORM's authors name "over-association of unrelated facts" as a core failure ([Shao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207)). So the link needs its own check. Checking that each endpoint is sourced is not enough.

**Verification has to check inference as inference.** Standard attribution checks (AIS, FActScore, SAFE, NLI) ask whether one source entails an atomic claim. AttributionBench's guidelines mark support that "requires significant interpretation or assumption" as not attributable ([AttributionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089)). Recent work names this problem "attribution myopia": one system scored 92.85% on attribution while its logical chain broke ([LogicScore, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15050)). MURGAT's answer is to identify which sentences carry directly observable claims and to score attribution only on those, so reasoning steps are not penalised ([MURGAT, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11509)). Put together, the design is:
1. Type each sentence as *stated*, *aggregated* or *inferred*.
2. For an inferred claim, require an explicit premise list.
3. Check each premise against its own quoted source.
4. Judge the inferential step separately for logical validity given those premises.
5. List the linchpin assumptions and what breaks if each one fails.
6. Display the claim with an ICD 203 likelihood band and a separate confidence level.

The GPT reviewer's caution belongs here, and it is correct. **"Not contradicted" is not "supported."** Finding no counter-evidence is not confirmation; Heuer makes the same point about deception, which by design leaves no evidence ([Heuer 1999](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The audit's own suggestion of a tier where the inference "only needs to be not contradicted" is too weak as written. The step check should require *positive* premise support and a *positive* validity judgment. Absence of contradiction is not enough. The labels must also stay distinct. A **"source-supported hypothesis"** is one whose premises are all sourced and whose mechanism is partly stated in sources. A **"new strategic insight / analytic judgment"** is one whose link is the system's own. The user's rule that "an insight backed by sources should be displayed" and the reviewer's rule that labels must not overclaim are compatible. Both are satisfied when every displayed judgment says which of these it is.

## Seven stacked gates turned every run since 10 October into an abstention

The delivered product contradicts the spec's own guarantees. The spec promises "never a blank report: landscape and briefing always; implications every run" and says "what credible experts or orgs say can always be shown" ([docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md:11-13, 35-41](docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md)). In the data, **every report after 10 Oct is "abstained"** ([data/reports.sqlite3](data/reports.sqlite3)). In the latest report (REPORT-02404), the briefing has an empty headline, 0 analogies, 0 cases and 0 expert commentary. Its policy fit says the advised organisation, OpenAI, was not addressed. Across the 20 reports since 9 Oct, expert commentary was non-empty twice and analogies three times, with one item each. Three reports on 23 Oct have zero implications and zero lab hypotheses. Two of them ended in about 21 seconds with `GeminiCreditsDepletedError`. The "never blank" rule therefore fails outright whenever a model or credit outage occurs.

**The gates are conjunctive, so near-zero output is the expected result.** The hidden-insight path passes through at least seven gates. The audit counts 16 fail-closed sites in a 23,259-line, 971 KB orchestrator ([services/research_orchestrator_service.py](services/research_orchestrator_service.py)). The gates are:
- **The claim-support gate.** A mechanism step that is not sourced is tolerated as an assumption only if at least 3 events and 2 publishers converge, nothing is contradicted and there are at most 3 assumptions. Otherwise it fails as "unsourced_steps_without_convergent_observations" ([services/claim_support_gate.py:72-97, 245-280](services/claim_support_gate.py)).
- **Four non-obviousness checks**, which fail closed.
- **The expert-frontier check.**
- **Counterevidence quality.**
- **The final quality assessment.**
- **The claim-supported release check.**
- **Policy enforcement.**

Actor moves add another bar. Crux, leverage, choice and move must each be grounded as "supported" or "inference_supported," and anything else is removed ([services/actor_moves.py:42-43, 420-476](services/actor_moves.py)). Removals in recent reports were 12, 24, 7, 7, 6, 8 and 6 items. In the latest run all six removals were OpenAI and Australian-taskforce leverage and move claims, which are exactly the product's purpose. The final rejection reasons point the same way. They are dominated by **mechanism_step_not_supported (15), conclusion_not_supported (15) and premise_not_supported (13)**. The first two reject inference for being inference. Only the third catches what is actually a hallucination risk.

Fairness requires one more point. The early published insights of 4–5 Oct passed only because mechanisms were not yet gated. Every mechanism step in REPORT-EE08E, 16D77 and 3C6F2 was "unsupported." The gates were added for a real reason: those insights were narrative without a traceable chain. The lesson is not to remove verification. It is to verify the right object, which is the premises and the step, rather than asking whether a source states the conclusion.

**The research layer loses or mislabels material before the gates even see it.** Seven defects stand out:

- **Fetch failures.** 21 of 51 gap-research sources on 23 Oct failed to fetch, and all 21 were vertexaisearch grounding-redirect URLs. Another 13 were never reviewed.
- **Off-target precedent queries.** The precedent search ran "epa 2015 waters of the united states rule impact" ten times for an AI-renaming development. The lab's research queries repeated three times each.
- **Commentary reclassified.** Expert commentary is reclassified as "reported" unless `claim_kind` is analysis or forecast ([services/hypothesis_evidence_service.py:730-733](services/hypothesis_evidence_service.py)). That explains why "experts always shown" almost never renders.
- **Lab fact cap.** The lab's generation prompt sees at most **40 of 191** facts, because of the default `limit=40` in `_facts_text` ([services/strategic_hypothesis_lab.py:134, 262-264](services/strategic_hypothesis_lab.py)).
- **Bare fact IDs.** Fact-ID bases such as "F7" are grounded literally as claims and fail ([services/strategic_hypothesis_lab.py:605-609](services/strategic_hypothesis_lab.py)).
- **Focus research arrives too late.** This audit finding needs a correction. The focus-organisation research *did* find the Capital One 2019 case: the log at 12:23 shows `capitalone.com/digital/facts2019` reviewed, with 1 accepted and 1 precedent, and actor_moves shows records 0 and past_cases 1. But `_actor_moves` runs after `_editorial_briefing`, which had already chosen its analogies at 12:21 ([services/research_orchestrator_service.py:3340-3372, 3894-3960](services/research_orchestrator_service.py)). `historical_analogies` is copied from the briefing, so a valid precedent was found and then discarded by stage ordering. That makes this the cheapest high-value fix in the codebase.
- **Inverted evaluator.** The evaluator ranks the reverse of what the product wants. It scored the "super intelligence" terminology-clash item **10 and both strategic-action items 0**, and only one pairwise comparison ran ([services/insight_evaluator.py:396-411](services/insight_evaluator.py)). The only "verified" lab hypothesis is a restatement of the reported event itself.

## Commercial tools stop at attribution, and tradecraft fills the gap with labels

Every market and threat-intelligence product reviewed follows the same pattern: a curated corpus, retrieval, an LLM summary, and a citation back to the source. AlphaSense Deep Research runs "dozens of searches" over 500M+ documents with "granular citations" ([AlphaSense](https://www.alpha-sense.com/press/alphasense-launches-deep-research-automating-in-depth-analysis-with-agentic-ai-on-high-value-content)). Bloomberg deliberately retreated from free-form synthesis toward "guiding analysts to relevant parts of call transcripts" to avoid hallucination ([Tech Brew](https://www.techbrew.com/stories/2024/02/02/bloomberg-ai-tool-quarterly-earnings)).

Recommendations, where they exist, are narrow:
- Klue's deal-level "landmines to set" ([Klue](https://klue.com/blog/compete-agent));
- Recorded Future's remediation playbooks ([Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/blog/next-evolution-recorded-future-ai-powering-threat-intelligence));
- Palantir's AI "proposals" staged for human approval ([Palantir AIP Logic](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/logic/overview/)).

Dataminr listed "recommended customer actions" in 2024 and "predictive capabilities that anticipate likely next steps" in 2025 as *future* features ([Dataminr](https://www.dataminr.com/press/dataminr-unveils-regenai-the-first-generative-ai-that-automatically-regenerates-in-real-time/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/29/dataminr-expands-intel-agents-bring-agentic-ai-physical-world)). No reviewed product publicly markets precedent matching, actor-leverage analysis or company-level strategic recommendations as AI output. That judgment layer stays with human analyst teams such as Insikt, Control Risks and Bloomberg Intelligence.

The reason matters for the app. **Citation linking verifies attribution, not inference.** Vendors keep output close to summary because their verification cannot cover anything else. Event Intelligence copied the vendors' verification model while pursuing a goal the vendors avoid. That is the root contradiction. No product found publicly labels claims as "stated" versus "inferred," including OpenAI, Gemini and Perplexity deep research. A product that does so credibly would lead the field rather than follow it. DeepResearch Bench also shows that citation precision and volume trade off across vendors ([DeepResearch Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11763)). Its FACT method extracts claim-source pairs, scrapes each source and has an LLM judge support. That method is a cheap regression metric the app can reuse for its *premise* layer.

Professional tradecraft has already solved the "never blank" and "expert views always shown" requirements. ICD 203 standard 8 says products "should not avoid difficult judgments in order to minimize the risk of being wrong." Standard 6 requires acknowledging "significant supporting and contrary information." Source summary statements say which sources matter most to each judgment ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). Heuer calls the alternative, describing the situation and "letting the intelligence consumer make the judgment," an evasion ([Heuer 1999](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The tradecraft answer to thin evidence is a low-confidence judgment that states its gaps, not silence. Precision also has measurable value: rounding 888,328 GJP forecasts into verbal bins reduced accuracy ([Friedman et al. 2018](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/value-precision-probability-assessment-evidence-large-scale-geopolitical-forecasting)). The app should keep numeric probabilities internally even when it displays words.

Hypothesis-generation research explains why single-item filters fail. LLM ideas were rated **more novel than expert ideas** by more than 100 blind reviewers ([Si et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)). That advantage vanished once 43 experts actually executed the ideas ([Ideation–Execution Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803)). Google's AI co-scientist does not filter each idea against a strict bar. It generates widely, ranks with pairwise Elo "debates," and evolves the leaders. Google itself calls Elo an auto-evaluation that needs external anchoring ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/)). For the app, this means:
- Keep candidates alive and rank them.
- Deduplicate before ranking.
- Use LLM judges to triage, not as absolute gates.
- Anchor on human ratings and on resolved outcomes.

Retrieval-augmented forecasters show where that anchoring leads. Halawi et al.'s system scored a Brier of 0.179 against the crowd's 0.149, and a blend of the two scored 0.146 ([Halawi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)). Leakage through date-filtered search routinely inflates backtests ([Paleka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723)). That bears directly on a system tuned by replaying a saved checkpoint.

## Four root causes explain why the app has not reached its objective

**First, the verification model contradicts the objective.** Original mechanism, move and leverage steps are graded on whether a source states them ([services/claim_support_gate.py:245-280](services/claim_support_gate.py); [services/actor_moves.py:42-43](services/actor_moves.py); [services/strategic_hypothesis_lab.py:590-615](services/strategic_hypothesis_lab.py)). RESEARCH_IMPLEMENTATION.md says outright that unsupported bridges "cannot earn mechanism support … merely because they are labelled assumptions" ([docs/RESEARCH_IMPLEMENTATION.md:23](docs/RESEARCH_IMPLEMENTATION.md)). By design, then, the spec's permission for labelled original insight cannot take effect. Pass rates multiply across gates, so seven or more gates produce near-zero output whatever the quality of the inputs.

**Second, the input is stale and narrow.** Every report since 9 Oct shares 185 of its roughly 190 evidence IDs with one checkpoint, `RUN-ED2409…` (63 events, 191 evidence records). The checkpoint was loaded four more times on 23 Oct. Meanwhile 1,502 events and 448 articles sit unused in `data/event_intelligence.db`, and the knowledge graph is empty ([data/checkpoints/](data/checkpoints/); [data/event_intelligence.db](data/event_intelligence.db)). Two weeks of v6.36.x tuning on one snapshot is overfitting by construction. Cross-actor and precedent material cannot grow because no new events arrive.

**Third, there is no ground truth.** There is no `insight_ratings.jsonl`. `user_feedback.sqlite3` holds 0 events, and the gold set has 7 items with 2 human pairs ([tests/data/insight_gold.json](tests/data/insight_gold.json)). RESEARCH_IMPLEMENTATION.md itself warns that offline tests "cover application behavior, not real-world insight quality" and recommends a human-judged set before more replays ([docs/RESEARCH_IMPLEMENTATION.md:47-51](docs/RESEARCH_IMPLEMENTATION.md)). Without that set, progress is measured by gate pass rates, and the gates are what produce the zeros. The measurement is circular.

**Fourth, the process only adds constraints.** The spec carries 49 v6.x versions, 11 of them tagged "GPT review." The orchestrator holds 92 `# v6.` patch comments. The repo root has four backup snapshots and three patch zips, and git holds only two commits. Each review adds a gate and none removes one. This is not a criticism of the reviews themselves. GPT's caution about "not contradicted" is right, and its two pending patches point the right way. The problem is that no external metric decides which constraints earn their place. Operations make it worse. A typical run costs about **33 minutes and about 1.27M prompt tokens across about 253 Gemini calls**, still abstains, and fails outright when credits run out. Seven replays of the same checkpoint ran in about 18 hours.

The two sides should be weighed fairly. The "loosen the gates" side is right that the current design cannot, even in principle, emit the spec's three output types. The "keep strict" side is right that the 4–5 Oct insights were unanchored narrative, and that labelling a weak inference "supported" would be worse than abstaining. A typed-claim design satisfies both sides only if it keeps two rules:
- premises must be positively sourced with exact quotes;
- the inferential step is judged on its validity, not on its absence of contradiction.

The **ranking_guard_patch** (exact source quotes required for inferential-bridge credit) supports the first rule, provided it applies to the bridge's *premises*. If it demands a quote for the bridge itself, it becomes an eighth gate of the kind that rejects inference for being inference.

**What to stop:**
- Stop replaying the single checkpoint.
- Stop adding a gate per review without a metric that justifies it. Each new constraint should have to show a gain on the human set, and each existing gate should be audited for its drop rate. That needs a per-gate funnel log (candidates in, passed, out), which does not exist today.
- Stop treating "no source states it" as a failure reason for conclusions and mechanism steps.
- Stop treating "no counter-evidence found" as support.
- Stop running roughly 33-minute, 1.3M-token runs whose output nobody rates.

**How to measure success.** Track three tiers:
- **Rendering compliance:** the user's rules hold in 100% of runs.
- **Quality:** human-rated usefulness of labelled judgments, with false-premise rate as the safety metric.
- **Calibration:** the Brier score of resolved forecasts.

Cost per run and the per-gate funnel are the operational guardrails.

## Conclusion

The app's ambition and its safeguards are not really in conflict. The conflict comes from applying a check built for attribution to claims that are inferences. The professional answer is the one ICD 203 gave a decade ago: label each claim, separate premises from judgment, state the linchpin assumptions and show confidence. Under that answer, original and well-supported insight is the expected output rather than a rule violation, and "not contradicted" never stands in for "supported." Commercial products have not crossed this line, so doing it carefully is a genuine differentiator, not an imitation.

The order of work matters more than any single fix. Without a human-rated set and fresh data, even a correct redesign cannot be told apart from another patch. The Capital One case shows how much is recoverable cheaply. The system already finds real precedents and loses them to plumbing, so the first gains come from fixing what is dropped, not from adding anything new.
